00Overview

This is the intelligence layer being built into UCLM (Unified Campaign Lifecycle Manager), and the trajectory is for it to become its own AI campaign product on top of UCLM. It isn't a fixed set of features: each module starts as its own PRD but is designed to plug into one connected system, with Agentic AI as the eventual orchestration layer.

01How the modules connect

Five recommendation factors (relevance, historical performance, business rules, content conflicts and guardrails) feed the Content AI decision. That joins the Time Affinity, Audience and Channel decisions, which all feed the Agentic AI orchestration layer. It plans the campaign and routes through human review before anything executes.

From module agents to autonomous Agentic AI

Module agents converge first into a Conversational Agentic AI (Stage 1): the growth manager states a goal in natural language, the agent plans and builds, and human review at launch is the only gate. Over time this can mature into an Autonomous Agentic AI (Stage 2, not yet built) that monitors live performance and relaunches improved configurations on its own.

02The principle, applied

Time Affinity

The human sets goals and constraints; the model recommends the best send window; the human can always override with a manual window.

Content AI

The human defines the campaign and audience; the model ranks and recommends content; the human approves before send.

Agentic AI

The human states the outcome in natural language; the agent plans and assembles the campaign; the human reviews and launches.

03Design process

This was the team's first AI initiative, so every open-forum discussion led to another round: diverge across possibilities, then converge on a cleaner flow for the growth manager (GM).

Product initially wanted the best possible recommendation engine regardless of UX cost: many required inputs, overwhelming transparency, and time and date bundled together. Design pushed back, arguing that an engine-first approach that overwhelms the user fails as a product regardless of model quality. Recommendation quality stayed with AI and engineering; design's job was to make that intelligence usable without asking the GM to configure all of it upfront.

Benchmarking. Flows were benchmarked against MoEngage and CleverTap to calibrate how much control and transparency enterprise marketers expect. The biggest insight: show peak engagement hours from historical data as a heatmap, scoped specifically to choosing a fallback time range. Every other AI send stays inside the regulatory do-not-disturb window.

04Leading the work

As Senior Lead, I decided how the work should be structured before deciding what it should be. I split ownership: one designer owns Time Affinity, another owns Content AI, each running their own iterations with weekly reviews.

That freed me to do what a designer buried in one module can't: hold the cross-module picture, manage stakeholders across product, engineering, the AI lead and business teams, and own the Agentic AI layer that only makes sense once you can see every piece.

The clearest example is the fallback-time concept. It wasn't in the original ask; it came from engaging business stakeholders during grooming. Stakeholder input also led us to drop a separate "delivery window" control, show the behaviour heatmap before asking for a fallback choice, and make the fallback-versus-AI split visually clear.

Steering the team's work

Resetting Time Affinity's direction. After two rounds of stakeholder review, the first direction wasn't landing. A request to switch between manual entry and AI had become a single two-way toggle, with no explanation of what each option meant and no visible reasoning, processing state or alternatives. I made the case to product for a per-module "enable AI" pattern instead of one whole-form toggle, and reassigned Time Affinity to the designer whose strengths fit edge-case-heavy work.

Resolving review questions through model logic, not taste.

  • The campaign time range and the fallback time were designed as two separate steps. I pushed for one shared time-range control serving both.
  • With cool-down periods (say, 4 messages a week with a 2-day cool-down), does the next message fire exactly at the boundary or any day after? Date Affinity picks the day on or after the boundary. Once that was explicit, we added help text wherever a GM would otherwise have to guess.

05Time Affinity AI

Design handed off

Problem. Campaigns are scheduled at the cohort level: one send time for everyone, regardless of individual behaviour. Every application decides timing on its own, timing experiments aren't automated the way content A/B tests are, and reporting doesn't show best-day or best-hour effects.

Vision. A central scheduling intelligence service that predicts each customer's best engagement window, keeps learning, and serves recommendations through reusable APIs to any Airtel Cloud product.

Phase 1. Live inside UCLM first, across Push, SMS, WhatsApp, Email, RCS, In-App and Journey channels, for one-time and recurring campaigns.

Why fallback ended up optional. Recurring schedules get complex fast: specific dates across months, different dates per month, fixed or varying times. After two or three iterations, the team chose to keep fallback optional so Phase 1 could ship and we could learn from real model behaviour before expanding.

06Content AI

In progress

Problem. Content selection is manual and disconnected from performance. Marketers pick from intuition or past campaigns, with no systematic way to rank options by relevance, predicted performance, or fit with business rules.

Vision. A layer that ranks, and eventually generates, content per customer, micro-cohort and channel, with the marketer in control of the final pick. It supports both a built-in model and bring-your-own-model for customers with their own trained models.

Capability framework

01SignalsBehavioural, transactional and engagement data train the model
02GenerationAI-assisted drafting of content variants
03LibraryA structured, taggable store of approved content
04RecommendationRanking by relevance, performance, rules, conflicts and guardrails

Guardrails cap duplication and stop the same or conflicting offers reaching a customer too close together.

Open design questions

  • How much ranking logic to expose versus a "why this?" explainer.
  • Where bring-your-own models plug into the same UI without a parallel flow.
  • How to make AI-generated drafts look clearly different from ranked existing assets.

07Agentic UCLM

Parked · POC validated

Instead of configuring each module separately, a growth manager states the outcome they want in natural language. Agentic AI plans and assembles the campaign using every module's intelligence, then hands back to the human for review and launch.

Today

Manual configuration of each module.

Stage 1

Conversational assembly, with human review at launch. Proof of concept validated.

Stage 2

Autonomous monitoring and relaunch. Future, not yet built.

A Beta PRD is scoped, but the work is paused until Time Affinity and Content AI ship and generate real usage data.
